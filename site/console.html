      where the name comes from
      

        
The giant had one vein, closed by a single nail.

        
Talos was a giant of bronze. Three times a day he circled Crete and let nothing ashore
        that did not belong there.

        
He is not remembered because he was strong. He is remembered because someone
        could stop him — and knew where.

        
Most agents build the strength first and the brake afterwards. This one was built the
        other way around.

      

    

    

      the nail · deterministic, no model in between
      

        
/stopHalts immediately. No model, no thinking round.

        
/undoTakes back the last action wherever it can be.

        
/autonomy 0Invalidates even rights already in flight.

        
/logWritten before the effect, not after.

        
A brake that has to think
        first is not a brake. A model that is hallucinating cannot prevent its own shutdown.

      known limits · stated first, not buried
      

        
A security claim without its limits is
        marketing. These are ours:

        
    
          
  • The shell needs a sandbox the platform can provide. bubblewrap on Linux,
          sandbox-exec on macOS. Where neither exists it refuses rather than running unprotected.
    • 
          
  • It is not a multi-tenant security boundary. One operator, one machine.
    • 
          
  • It does not defend against a malicious model. It defends against a mistaken
          one, and against injection arriving through tool output. Different threats.
    • 
          
  • Every way in has to fetch, never receive. Telegram long-polls, mail is
          pulled over IMAP — nothing opens a listening socket. A From: header is
          only a claim; unauthenticated mail gets ask-only trust — it may ask, it may never approve.
    •

There is no
        default allowlist. TALOS_ALLOWED_PRINCIPALS must name your identity or
        nothing starts — a shipped default would be a backdoor printed in the source. Setup reads
        your id from a message you send your own bot: the one identity it trusts is one you proved
        you control. Python 3.11+, runs on a Raspberry Pi.
